## CHEST EXERCISES

### Bench Press
Primary muscles: Pectoralis major, triceps, anterior deltoid
Secondary muscles: Serratus anterior, biceps
Sets: 3-4 | Reps: 8-12 | Rest: 90 seconds
Form tips:
- Retract shoulder blades and keep them pinned to the bench
- Feet flat on the floor throughout the movement
- Bar path slightly diagonal — lower to mid chest, press up and back
- Keep wrists straight, elbows at 45-75 degrees from body
- Control the descent — 2 seconds down, explosive up
Beginner tip: Start with an empty bar or dumbbells until form is perfect. Ego lifting causes shoulder injuries.
Common mistake: Flaring elbows out to 90 degrees — this puts excessive stress on the shoulder joint.

### Incline Dumbbell Press
Primary muscles: Upper pectoralis major, anterior deltoid
Sets: 3 | Reps: 10-12 | Rest: 75 seconds
Form tips:
- Set bench to 30-45 degrees — higher angles shift work to shoulders
- Lower dumbbells to chest level, elbows slightly below shoulder height
- Press up and slightly inward — dumbbells nearly touch at top
Beginner tip: Great for developing the upper chest which gives a full, complete look for bodybuilding.

### Cable Chest Fly
Primary muscles: Pectoralis major (emphasis on stretch)
Sets: 3 | Reps: 12-15 | Rest: 60 seconds
Form tips:
- Slight forward lean, slight bend in elbows throughout
- Feel the stretch at the bottom — this is where muscle growth happens
- Squeeze hard at the top, hold for 1 second
Beginner tip: Use lighter weight and focus on the muscle contraction, not moving the weight.

### Push Up
Primary muscles: Pectoralis major, triceps, core
Sets: 3 | Reps: 10-20 | Rest: 60 seconds
Form tips:
- Straight line from head to heel — no sagging hips or raised glutes
- Elbows at 45 degrees from body, not flared out
- Full range of motion — chest touches or nearly touches floor
Beginner tip: If too hard, start with knees on floor. If too easy, elevate feet or add a weighted vest.

## BACK EXERCISES

### Lat Pulldown
Primary muscles: Latissimus dorsi, biceps, rear deltoid
Sets: 3-4 | Reps: 10-12 | Rest: 75 seconds
Form tips:
- Grip slightly wider than shoulder width
- Pull bar to upper chest, not behind neck
- Lean back slightly — about 15-20 degrees
- Lead with elbows, not hands — think "elbows to hips"
- Slow and controlled on the way back up
Beginner tip: This is the best exercise to build the V-taper that is essential for bodybuilding.

### Seated Cable Row
Primary muscles: Middle trapezius, rhomboids, latissimus dorsi, biceps
Sets: 3 | Reps: 10-12 | Rest: 75 seconds
Form tips:
- Sit tall, slight arch in lower back
- Pull handle to lower chest/upper abdomen
- Squeeze shoulder blades together at the end of the movement
- Do not round the back or use momentum
Beginner tip: Think about pulling your elbows behind your body, not just pulling the weight.

### Dumbbell Row
Primary muscles: Latissimus dorsi, rhomboids, biceps
Sets: 3 | Reps: 10-12 per side | Rest: 60 seconds
Form tips:
- Brace one hand and knee on a bench
- Keep back flat and parallel to floor
- Pull dumbbell to hip, not shoulder
- Elbow stays close to body throughout
Beginner tip: One of the best unilateral exercises — helps fix left/right muscle imbalances.

## SHOULDER EXERCISES

### Dumbbell Shoulder Press
Primary muscles: Anterior and medial deltoid, triceps
Sets: 3-4 | Reps: 10-12 | Rest: 75 seconds
Form tips:
- Start with dumbbells at ear height, elbows at 90 degrees
- Press straight up, dumbbells nearly touch at top
- Do not lock elbows at the top
- Lower slowly — 2-3 seconds down
Beginner tip: Seated version is safer for beginners as it removes the need for core stabilization.

### Lateral Raise
Primary muscles: Medial deltoid (side shoulder)
Sets: 3 | Reps: 12-15 | Rest: 60 seconds
Form tips:
- Slight bend in elbows throughout
- Raise arms to shoulder height — no higher
- Lead with elbows, not wrists
- Lower slowly — the eccentric phase builds muscle
- Avoid shrugging — keep traps relaxed
Beginner tip: Use very light weight. This is an isolation exercise — form matters more than load.
Common mistake: Using momentum and swinging the weight up. Slow down.

### Face Pull
Primary muscles: Rear deltoid, external rotators, trapezius
Sets: 3 | Reps: 15-20 | Rest: 60 seconds
Form tips:
- Set cable at face height or slightly above
- Pull rope to face, hands ending beside ears
- Elbows flare out and back
- Pause and squeeze at the end
Beginner tip: Critical for shoulder health and rear deltoid development. Often neglected but essential for bodybuilding symmetry.

## LEG EXERCISES

### Squat
Primary muscles: Quadriceps, glutes, hamstrings, core
Sets: 3-4 | Reps: 8-12 | Rest: 2 minutes
Form tips:
- Feet shoulder width apart, toes slightly out
- Brace core before descending
- Knees track over toes — do not cave inward
- Descend until thighs parallel to floor or below
- Drive through heels on the way up
- Keep chest up and back straight throughout
Beginner tip: Master the bodyweight squat before adding any load. Mobility work for ankles and hips helps significantly.
Common mistake: Knees caving in on the ascent — this causes long-term knee damage.

### Leg Press
Primary muscles: Quadriceps, glutes, hamstrings
Sets: 3 | Reps: 10-15 | Rest: 90 seconds
Form tips:
- Feet shoulder width apart, mid-platform
- Lower until knees reach 90 degrees — do not let lower back peel off the pad
- Do not lock knees at the top
- Higher foot placement emphasizes glutes, lower emphasizes quads
Beginner tip: Good alternative if squat form needs work. Allows heavier loading with less technical demand.

### Romanian Deadlift
Primary muscles: Hamstrings, glutes, lower back
Sets: 3 | Reps: 10-12 | Rest: 90 seconds
Form tips:
- Start standing with dumbbells or barbell in front of thighs
- Hinge at hips, push hips back, slight bend in knees
- Lower weight along legs, feel the hamstring stretch
- Drive hips forward to return to standing
Beginner tip: Feel the stretch in the hamstrings at the bottom — that is the range of motion you want to work in.

## ARM EXERCISES

### Barbell Curl
Primary muscles: Biceps brachii, brachialis
Sets: 3 | Reps: 10-12 | Rest: 60 seconds
Form tips:
- Grip shoulder width, elbows pinned to sides
- Curl to shoulder height — do not swing
- Squeeze at the top, lower slowly
- Do not let elbows drift forward
Beginner tip: Supinate the wrist as you curl — rotate palms upward. This maximizes bicep activation.

### Tricep Pushdown
Primary muscles: Triceps brachii (all three heads)
Sets: 3 | Reps: 12-15 | Rest: 60 seconds
Form tips:
- Elbows pinned to sides, do not let them flare
- Push straight down, full extension at bottom
- Slow return — resist the weight on the way up
Beginner tip: Triceps make up 2/3 of upper arm size. If you want bigger arms, prioritize triceps.

### Hammer Curl
Primary muscles: Brachialis, brachioradialis, biceps
Sets: 3 | Reps: 10-12 | Rest: 60 seconds
Form tips:
- Neutral grip — thumbs facing up throughout
- Same curl motion as barbell curl
- Develops the brachialis which pushes the bicep up for a peaked look
Beginner tip: Alternating arms allows heavier weight and more focus per rep.

## CORE EXERCISES

### Plank
Primary muscles: Transverse abdominis, rectus abdominis, obliques, glutes
Sets: 3 | Duration: 30-60 seconds | Rest: 45 seconds
Form tips:
- Forearms on floor, elbows under shoulders
- Straight line from head to heel
- Squeeze glutes and core simultaneously
- Do not let hips sag or rise
Beginner tip: Quality over duration. A 20-second perfect plank beats a 60-second sagging one.

### Leg Raise
Primary muscles: Lower rectus abdominis, hip flexors
Sets: 3 | Reps: 12-15 | Rest: 60 seconds
Form tips:
- Lie flat, hands under glutes for lower back support
- Keep legs straight, raise to 90 degrees
- Lower slowly — do not let feet touch the floor
- Control the movement — no swinging

## WORKOUT STRUCTURE & PROGRAMMING

### Sets and Reps Guidelines
- Strength (1-5 reps): Heavy weight, long rest (3-5 min), builds raw strength
- Hypertrophy/Muscle Building (6-12 reps): Moderate weight, 60-90 sec rest — BEST for bodybuilding
- Endurance (15+ reps): Light weight, short rest (30-45 sec), builds muscle endurance
- For beginners: Start in the 10-12 rep range with 3 sets per exercise

### Rest Between Sets
- Compound exercises (squat, bench, deadlift): 90 seconds to 2 minutes
- Isolation exercises (curls, lateral raises): 60 seconds
- Heavy strength work: Up to 3-5 minutes
- Reducing rest increases metabolic stress — good for conditioning but reduces strength performance

### Training Frequency
- Each muscle group needs 48-72 hours recovery before training again
- Beginners: Full body 3x per week OR upper/lower 4x per week
- Intermediate: Push/Pull/Legs 6x per week or body part split
- Training a muscle group 2x per week is the minimum effective frequency for muscle growth

### Muscle Group Order (Within a Session)
1. Large compound movements first (squat, bench, deadlift, rows)
2. Smaller compound movements second (shoulder press, dips, pull-ups)
3. Isolation exercises last (curls, lateral raises, leg extensions)
Reason: You have the most energy and neuromuscular activation at the start of a session.

### Progressive Overload
The #1 principle of muscle growth. You must continuously increase the challenge over time.
Methods:
- Add weight (2.5-5kg when you can complete all reps with good form)
- Add reps (work from 8 to 12 before increasing weight)
- Add sets (progress from 3 to 4 sets)
- Reduce rest time
- Increase range of motion
Without progressive overload, your body adapts and muscle growth stops.

## NUTRITION FOR MUSCLE BUILDING

### Protein
- Target: 1.8-2.2g per kg of bodyweight per day
- For Apaar (88kg): approximately 160-195g protein per day
- Best sources: chicken breast, eggs, Greek yogurt, cottage cheese, fish, lean beef, whey protein
- Spread across 4-5 meals for optimal muscle protein synthesis
- Each meal should contain at least 30-40g protein

### Caloric Surplus for Muscle Gain
- Eat 250-500 calories above your maintenance level
- Too large a surplus = excessive fat gain
- Too small a surplus = slow muscle gain
- Track progress: aim for 0.25-0.5kg weight gain per week as a beginner

### Carbohydrates
- Primary fuel source for training
- Eat carbs around training — before for energy, after for recovery
- Best sources: rice, oats, sweet potato, fruit, whole grain bread
- Do not eliminate carbs — they are essential for performance and recovery

### Hydration
- Drink at least 3-4 litres of water per day
- Dehydration reduces strength by up to 15%
- Drink 500ml water 30 minutes before training
- Sip water throughout the session

### Meal Timing
- Pre-workout (60-90 min before): Carbs + moderate protein, low fat
- Post-workout (within 60 min): Protein + carbs to maximize recovery
- Before bed: Slow-digesting protein (cottage cheese, casein) to support overnight muscle repair

## INJURY PREVENTION & RECOVERY

### Warm Up Protocol
- 5-10 min light cardio to raise heart rate and body temperature
- Dynamic stretching — leg swings, arm circles, hip rotations
- Warm up sets: 2 sets at 50% and 75% of working weight before your first exercise
- Never skip the warm up — cold muscles tear more easily

### Common Beginner Injuries and Prevention
- Shoulder impingement: Caused by poor bench press form and neglecting rear delts. Fix: Face pulls, proper bench technique.
- Lower back strain: Caused by rounded back on deadlifts and rows. Fix: Brace core, hip hinge pattern.
- Knee pain: Caused by knees caving on squats. Fix: Strengthen glutes, knees-over-toes technique.
- Bicep tendon strain: Caused by ego curling too heavy. Fix: Lighter weight, full range of motion.

### Recovery
- Sleep 7-9 hours per night — this is when muscle growth actually happens
- Active recovery on rest days: walking, light stretching, mobility work
- Muscle soreness (DOMS) peaks 24-72 hours after training — normal, not dangerous
- Pain in joints or sharp pain = stop immediately, do not train through it

### Deload Week
- Every 6-8 weeks, reduce volume by 40-50% for one week
- Allows connective tissue, joints, and nervous system to recover
- You will come back stronger — not weaker
- Signs you need a deload: persistent fatigue, strength dropping, poor sleep, no motivation to train
